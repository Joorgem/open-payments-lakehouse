# ADR 0014 — the star is grained on `empresa`, and the spec's `dim_merchant` cannot be joined to

## Status
Accepted. Implemented as `DIM_COMPANY` in `src/opl/gold/registry.py`, built by
`src/opl/gold/dimensions.py` and reached twice per fact row (`payer_company_sk`,
`payee_company_sk`) by `src/opl/gold/facts.py`. Written in F3 after the layer
existed: the ruling was argued in the phase plan, restated in module docstrings
and in `databricks/resources/gold_dim_company_job.yml`, and had **no findable
home** — a reader arriving from [ADR 0011](0011-no-hub-socio-and-a-disappearance-driven-effectivity-satellite.md),
whose Consequences forbid a `dim_socio`, had nothing to follow forward to. That
is the gap this file closes.

**What it decides.** Which entity the dimensional layer is grained on, and
therefore which of the governing spec's §4.3 dimensions are buildable at all.
**Three** of the dimensions §4.3 asks for are absent — `dim_merchant`,
`dim_geography` and `dim_customer` — and a fourth, `dim_socio`, is absent because
an earlier ADR **forbids** it rather than because §4.3 asked for it. Each is
absent for a different reason and **only two of the four are deferrals**, which is
why they are tabulated below instead of listed. The §4.3 dimensions this star does
build — `dim_date`, `dim_channel`, `dim_currency` — are conformed and are not in
scope here.

## Context

### The fact's counterparties are eight characters, and eight characters do not name an establishment

`opl.contracts.payments` draws both counterparties from `cnpj_basico` — the
**8-character CNPJ root**, which is `hub_empresa`'s business key. The reason is
recorded there and is about resolution rather than convenience: both sides come
out of the real hub key space, so 100% of them resolve by construction and any
rate below 100% is a finding rather than a tolerance.

The spec's `dim_merchant` is SCD2 at **estabelecimento** grain — the 14-character
CNPJ, `(cnpj_basico, cnpj_ordem, cnpj_dv)`. The two grains are not the same
entity, and the measured cardinalities say why a join between them is undefined:

| hub | rows | source |
|---|---|---|
| `hub_empresa` | **69,062,849** | `docs/f2-wave-1-workspace-run-evidence.md` §1.1 |
| `hub_estabelecimento` | **72,318,968** | same, §2.1 |

**72,318,968 / 69,062,849 = 1.047 establishments per company.** The mapping is
one-to-many in exactly the direction that breaks the lookup: given a `cnpj_basico`
there is on average more than one establishment and **no rule in this vault for
choosing between them**. The join is not expensive or awkward; it has no answer.
`link_empresa_estabelecimento` holds 72,318,968 rows for the same reason — the
link's grain is the child's, because the hierarchy has one parent per child and
not one child per parent.

### The option the plan did not consider, and it is the real one

`src/opl/contracts/payments.py:108-117` argues the case against a 14-character
counterparty, and argues it correctly — but against the wrong alternative. It
weighs **inventing** `cnpj_ordem` and `cnpj_dv` beside a real root, and rejects it
on the grounds that an establishment whose root resolves while the establishment
itself does not exist is "a weaker claim wearing a longer number": it would
resolve against `hub_empresa` and fail against `hub_estabelecimento`. That is
right, and it disposes of a straw man.

The option it does not consider is **drawing the counterparty pool from
`hub_estabelecimento` in the first place** — real 14-character keys, sampled from
the real establishment hub exactly as the current pool is sampled from the real
company hub. Nothing about it is unsound. Every property `payments.py` defends
survives it: the keys are real, 100% resolve by construction, and a rate below
100% stays a finding.

**What it costs is a full F1b re-run, and this ADR states that plainly rather
than filing it as a widening.** The counterparty pool is an input to stream
generation, so changing it changes every generated byte: new `transaction_id`s,
new payment rows, new bronze files, **new digests**, and a new resolution
measurement to replace the 1,024-of-1,024 figure the current claim rests on. It
is not a column widening and it is not a migration — it is the payment side of
the lakehouse regenerated from its first step, with every published number
downstream of it re-measured.

### `dim_geography` fails on the grain **and** on something the plan missed

The phase plan records `dim_geography` as blocked by the same grain problem: an
address is an attribute of an establishment, and the fact cannot reach one. That
is true and sufficient.

There is a second obstruction, and it is worth recording because it survives even
if the grain problem is solved by a different route. The obvious rescue is to
roll a company's address up from its **matriz** — the head establishment — which
would give a company-grained geography without any 14-character key in the fact.
**This vault cannot identify a matriz.** `identificador_matriz_filial` is
declared in `UNMODELLED_ESTABELECIMENTO_COLUMNS`
(`src/opl/vault/domains/cnpj.py:212-216`) with the reason: it is an attribute of
the **relationship**, not of the establishment alone, so it belongs on a satellite
of `link_empresa_estabelecimento`, which this vault has no shape for yet. Without
it, "the company's address" is a choice among 1.047 addresses with nothing in the
vault to make it. `sat_estabelecimento_endereco` holds 72,888,582 rows and none of
them is marked as the one to roll up.

### The other two are not deferrals at all

- **`dim_customer`** — there is no customer entity in this lakehouse. `hub_customer`
  is F2 wave 2 and does not exist. A dimension over a hub that has not been built
  is out of scope, not deferred.
- **`dim_socio`** — **forbidden**, by
  [ADR 0011](0011-no-hub-socio-and-a-disappearance-driven-effectivity-satellite.md).
  The RFB masks partner CPFs to six middle digits over a 10^6 space that is
  99.99% saturated, so `cpf_cnpj_socio` identifies a **partnership**, never a
  partner. That ADR's Consequences say it directly: *"The dimensional layer must
  not build a `dim_socio` on `cpf_cnpj_socio`; the honest artefact is a
  partnership bridge."* This is the layer that sentence was addressed to, and it
  complies. The bridge is not built either — building it needs `load_satellite`
  to accept a link, which ADR 0011 already records as a real edit rather than a
  copy.

## Options considered

### 1. `dim_merchant` at estabelecimento grain, as §4.3 asks

**Rejected as unbuildable against this fact, not as unwanted.** With counterparties
that are 8 characters, the fact has no column that names an establishment. Any
join would have to pick one of a company's establishments, and picking amounts to
inventing a fact about the payment that the payment does not carry — a merchant
attribution that looks precise and is arbitrary. A star whose foreign key means
"one of these, we don't know which" is worse than one grained where the key is
exact, because the imprecision is invisible in every downstream query.

### 2. `dim_merchant` reached by inventing `cnpj_ordem`/`cnpj_dv` on the existing roots

**Rejected**, on `payments.py`'s own argument: the invented establishment resolves
against `hub_empresa` and fails against `hub_estabelecimento`, so the star gains a
14-character key and *loses* the 100%-resolution property that is the integration
claim. Longer number, weaker claim.

### 3. Re-draw the counterparty pool from `hub_estabelecimento`, then build `dim_merchant`

**The honest fuller version, and deferred rather than rejected.** It is sound; it
is also a **full F1b re-run** — new bytes, new digests, a new resolution
measurement — and it re-opens every published payment-side number in
`docs/f1b-run-evidence.md` and `docs/f3-run-evidence.md`. Deferring it costs
nothing that cannot be recovered: `dim_company` is not a step toward it that would
have to be unwound, because a `dim_merchant` at establishment grain is a
**different dimension over a different hub**, not a widening of this one.

### 4. `dim_company` at empresa grain, two role-playing keys into it

**Chosen.** The fact's counterparty column IS `hub_empresa`'s business key, so the
as-of lookup is exact, resolves for 100% of references by construction, and the
role-playing pattern (`payer_company_sk`, `payee_company_sk` into one dimension)
is the Kimball demonstration §4.3 is actually asking for — a fact reaching one
conformed dimension twice, each reference resolved independently as of the
payment's own instant.

## Decision

**The MLV star is grained on `empresa`. One SCD2 `dim_company` over
`sat_empresa_dados`, reached twice by `fact_payment` as payer and payee. No
`dim_merchant`, no `dim_geography`, no `dim_customer`, no `dim_socio`.**

The four absences are recorded with a status and an unblocking condition each —
no two of the conditions are the same — because collapsing them into one
"deferred" list is what made this ruling unfindable in the first place. Only the
first three are §4.3's asks; `dim_socio` is in the table because a reader looking
for it deserves to find it here rather than in an ADR about the vault:

| absent dimension | status | what would unblock it |
|---|---|---|
| `dim_merchant` (SCD2, estabelecimento) | **deferred** | a full F1b re-run drawing the pool from `hub_estabelecimento` |
| `dim_geography` (estabelecimento address) | **deferred, twice blocked** | the above, **or** a satellite on `link_empresa_estabelecimento` carrying `identificador_matriz_filial` |
| `dim_customer` (SCD2) | **out of scope** | `hub_customer`, which is F2 wave 2 |
| `dim_socio` | **forbidden** ([ADR 0011](0011-no-hub-socio-and-a-disappearance-driven-effectivity-satellite.md)) | nothing — the RFB withheld the identity |

**`hub_merchant` is a different merchant, and a reader searching this repository for
"merchant" finds both.**
[ADR 0017](0017-a-postgres-snapshot-diff-and-the-key-that-is-not-in-the-payload.md)
Decision 5 keys `hub_merchant` on the F-DB Postgres registry's own `merchant_id` and reaches
the company through a link; the `dim_merchant` deferred above is the spec's
*estabelecimento*-grained SCD2 dimension over Receita CNPJ data. Building one does not
unblock the other — this row's condition is still the F1b re-run named beside it.

## Consequences

- **The star's headline claim is narrower than §4.3's and is exactly true.** It
  demonstrates SCD2, role-playing dimensions, conformed dimensions and an as-of
  join at 69.2M-row scale. It does not demonstrate a merchant hierarchy, and this
  file is where that is said rather than left to be noticed.
- **`hub_estabelecimento` and its two satellites are built and unused by the
  star.** That is not waste: `pit_estabelecimento` is built over them
  ([ADR 0013](0013-two-satellites-on-hub-estabelecimento.md)), and it is the only
  place in this lakehouse where two satellites with independent timelines meet one
  hub. The gold layer therefore has two demonstrations with **no join between
  them**, which is a real seam and is named here so nobody looks for the join.
- **The 1.047 ratio is a property of this snapshot pair, not a law.** It is low
  enough that "a company is roughly its establishment" is tempting, and that is
  precisely the temptation this ADR exists to refuse: 1.047 average with no rule
  for the excess is still no rule.
- **Deferring `dim_merchant` keeps `dim_company` cheap to keep.** It is not a
  prototype of the merchant dimension and does not become dead weight if one is
  ever built — a payer/payee pair at company grain is a legitimate model of a
  company-to-company payment, which is what this stream generates.
- **ADR 0011's forward reference now resolves.** Its Consequences addressed a
  dimensional layer that did not exist when it was written; that layer exists now
  and complies, and 0011 links here.

### What would change this decision

- Payments whose counterparties are genuinely 14 characters — a real feed, or the
  F1b re-run of option 3. Then `dim_merchant` is buildable and this ADR is
  superseded rather than amended, because the star's grain changes.
- A satellite on `link_empresa_estabelecimento` carrying
  `identificador_matriz_filial`. That alone unblocks a company-grained
  `dim_geography` **without** touching the fact, which makes it the cheapest of
  the two paths and the one to take first if either is taken.
- A customer entity arriving in F2 wave 2. `dim_customer` then becomes a
  deferral rather than out of scope, and this table gets a fifth row.
